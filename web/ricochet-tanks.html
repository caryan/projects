How to play, and how it works

Rules. Each round starts in a new random maze. Shells bounce off walls and last about nine seconds, so a miss can come back for you: your own shells can hit you too, and a shot straight at a nearby wall comes straight back. Each tank can have five shells in the air at once. When a tank is hit, the round ends a couple of seconds later: if the other tank is still standing it scores a point, and if both were hit it's a draw.

The maze. It starts as a grid of closed cells. A random depth-first walk knocks down walls to join every cell into one winding maze with exactly one route between any two cells. Then about a quarter of the remaining inner walls are knocked out at random, which adds loops and gives shells more lines to travel along.

Bouncing shells. Each wall is a thin rectangle. Every tick, a shell's path is traced as a ray against the walls, each grown by the shell's radius, and the first wall it would cross reflects it. That repeats for the rest of the tick's distance, so a fast shell can never slip through a wall or a corner. A shell is fired from the tank's centre and traced out to the muzzle in the same way, so a barrel poking through a wall can't fire through it.

Two laptops. The laptops talk directly over WebRTC, paired with the site's lib/qr-pair.js. The host's laptop runs the whole game. The other laptop sends just its keys, about 60 times a second, and draws what the host sends back: tank and shell positions about 60 times a second on the fast channel, and each new maze and the scores on the reliable one. The guest draws a few hundredths of a second behind, blending between the last two updates so motion stays smooth through a lost packet. If the link drops, Pair again reconnects without reloading and the match carries on.
